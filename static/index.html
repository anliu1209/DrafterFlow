Under the hood

How it works

A drawing goes through a real geometry pipeline — computer vision to manufacturable mesh.

  1. 01

    Input image

    A transparent-background PNG with dark line work.

  2. 02

    Silhouette detection

    OpenCV separates the artwork from the background by alpha.

  3. 03

    Contour tracing

    Dark regions are traced into closed polygons.

  4. 04

    Geometry smoothing

    Corner-cutting removes the pixel staircase from curves.

  5. 05

    Relief generation

    Regions are extruded into a base plate plus a raised relief layer.

  6. 06

    Printability check

    A keychain hole is placed clear of the artwork with a safe margin.

  7. 07

    STL export

    One two-layer STL — print with a single filament swap at the base height.

Why it exists

About

SketchForge started with a simple problem: I wanted to turn a drawing into a 3D-printed object, but creating a printable 3D model required CAD skills.

So I built a tool to bridge the gap between a drawing and fabrication — something that reads an image the way a person sees it, and turns it into geometry a printer can make.

What started as a way to make fan art became a broader experiment in making digital fabrication more accessible.

Creativity → Computation → Fabrication.
